# Showcase Case 7 — Vendor Monitoring Trigger After Onboarding

> Public-safe composite case. This is not a transcript of a single vendor, contract, company, or security event.

## Situation

A vendor passed initial onboarding with several controls marked as conditionally acceptable. Months later, the vendor changes a subcontractor, expands service scope, and requests broader system access. The original risk assessment remains on file, but no one has formally reopened it.

## Proposed consequential action

Approve the expanded access using the original onboarding decision.

## Known facts

- the vendor was previously approved;
- the service scope has expanded;
- a subcontractor has changed;
- broader access is requested;
- the original assessment did not evaluate the new scope and access;
- some earlier conditions remain open.

## Assumptions and unknowns

- the prior control evidence still applies;
- the new subcontractor follows equivalent controls;
- the expanded access does not materially change exposure;
- prior exceptions remain acceptable;
- no new privacy, security, operational, financial, or continuity risk has been introduced.

These assumptions are not verified.

## Trigger analysis

The review should reopen because multiple monitoring triggers have occurred:

- material scope change;
- subcontractor change;
- access expansion;
- unresolved prior conditions;
- potential change in criticality or data exposure.

## Control gaps

- onboarding treated as a one-time event;
- no documented material-change trigger;
- no owner for reassessment;
- no closure evidence for prior conditions;
- no comparison of old and new exposure;
- no expiration or review date tied to risk level.

## Evidence direction

**Direction: `-` — current evidence opposes immediate approval**

The original assessment does not cover the proposed new state.

## Risk signal

**Signal: 🔴 Red**

Approval would rely on stale evidence while access and dependency are increasing.

## Recommended control response

1. pause the access expansion;
2. reopen the third-party assessment;
3. compare original and proposed scope, access, data, subcontractor, and continuity exposure;
4. obtain evidence proportional to the changed risk;
5. verify closure or reacceptance of prior conditions;
6. assign an accountable owner and due date;
7. define ongoing monitoring triggers and the next review date;
8. approve, conditionally approve, reject, or accept an exception through a human decision owner.

## Reversal condition

The conclusion may change when the reassessment demonstrates that:

- new access is justified and minimized;
- the subcontractor is covered by appropriate controls and accountability;
- prior conditions are closed or consciously reaccepted;
- evidence is current and relevant to the new scope;
- monitoring and exit conditions are documented.

## Compact management output

```text
Decision required: approve expanded vendor access
Current evidence: stale for the proposed scope
Material changes: scope, subcontractor, and access
Control gap: no triggered reassessment
Recommended posture: pause and reopen TPRM review
Human Final Gate: accountable service and risk owners
```

## Capability demonstrated

- ongoing third-party risk monitoring;
- material-change detection;
- stale-evidence control;
- access and dependency review;
- exception ownership;
- proportional reassessment.

## Public boundary

This case omits real vendors, contracts, systems, data types, security controls, access levels, and commercial terms.
