# 29 — Proposal–Execution–Reality Verification Gap (PERV-G)

**Status:** User-authorized method-level Full-Push / Not Frozen (2026-11-14). Public-safe conceptual method. Incremental to Reality Gate, SREG, HCRS-G, CEED-G, OCAA and Human Final. Not a production runtime, formal Bridge-0 grammar, or manufacturing certification.

## Core distinctions

- Proposal != Engineering Feasibility != Attempted Trial != Qualified Production.
- Plausible 3-D/visual representation != Validated physical dynamics or safe assembly.
- Schematic/simulator consistency != As-built wiring or operating truth.
- Successful press-fit != Proven retention, functional response, yield or safety.
- One shop's difficulty != Universal inability; one shop's success != Repeatable economical production.
- Apparent low-order economics != Verified supplier motive; actual field observation != Certified measurement.
- A second manufacturer's result may test a technical alternative; it does not reveal the first manufacturer's private intent.

## Evidence-driven field-validation workflow

1. **State competing hypotheses.** Separate process/tooling difficulty, order size, material, fixture, specification, commercial incentives and communication failures. Keep attribution and uncertainty.
2. **Bind the physical problem.** Confirm part revision, sample provenance, geometry and material assumptions, interface dimensions, constraints, owner, human authorization and safety consequences. Mark missing quantities as unknown.
3. **Define independent observations.** Specify test specimens, pre/post sample condition, instrumented measurements, success/failure oracle, acceptable operating envelope and stop conditions before testing.
4. **Look at the real system.** Keep AI-generated designs, digital twin visuals, schematics and simulators as aids; actual worksite observations and independent measurements are separate evidence tiers.
5. **Locate the failure stage.** Record whether the problem occurs in alignment, insertion, deformation, retention, operation, repeatability or cost. Avoid guessing that a workshop is unwilling because it did not succeed.
6. **Replicate with controlled differences.** When permitted, compare a qualified second supplier or alternative fixture using traceable specimens and declared changes; preserve an untouched reference where practicable.
7. **Separate acceptance gates.** Technical assembly, independent function/safety qualification, repeatability/yield, capital/tooling requirements and order economics are different decisions.
8. **Human stop/restart.** Pause spending without discarding parts, source data or optional cooperation; restart only with bounded evidence and authorized scope.

## Anonymous illustrative case

A small industrial retaining element looked straightforward in a representation but was difficult to insert using a workshop's available process. An observer saw genuine attempts, thereby weakening a prior guess that the workshop simply did not want a small order. This is an observed **local engineering difficulty**; it is not proof that all manufacturers fail, that the workshop's internal motives are known, or that the device meets safety requirements. Historical bench records and an independent qualified second workshop are possible next verification steps; no outcome is assumed.

For hydraulic safety components, relevant future checks may include force–displacement, installed depth, retention under operating conditions, leakage, functional flow response, repeatability and hazard review. These are proposed dimensions, not completed tests or instructions for unsupervised use.

## Cross-domain transfer

- Electrical service: drawings and simulators may disagree with actual wiring.
- AI spatial generation: visual coherence is not automatically action-conditioned physics.
- AI coding and release: generated patch != verified change != observed production outcome.
- Human collaboration: replace motive stories with reported behavior, evidence and alternative hypotheses.

## Negative test designs (NOT executed field tests)

1. Rendered assembly only -> physical feasibility unverified.
2. Single failed supplier trial -> no claim of universal impossibility.
3. Successful second supplier with different fixture -> first supplier motive still unknown.
4. Component pressed in but no function/retention evidence -> safety release HOLD.
5. Diagram and simulator agree, field differs -> preserve and investigate discrepancy.
6. Small order assumed unprofitable without records -> commercial hypothesis only.
7. Samples retained for possible future trials -> no implied order or ownership transfer.

**Publication boundary:** No supplier identities, personal contacts, raw photos, proprietary geometry, unpublished test data or identifiable incident narrative. No Bridge-0 runtime/model-weight change, automatic deployment, lifting of security HOLD, or new version-bridge confirmation.
