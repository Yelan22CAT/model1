# Claim-to-Evidence and Summary Fidelity Review

**Type:** public-safe method for reviewing technical communication. **Status:** illustrative review checklist, not a truth engine, proprietary prompt reconstruction, model fingerprint or production validator. Last reviewed: 2026-09-19.

Strong titles and compressed conclusions help people navigate technical information, but a claim may become misleading when a source-specific limitation disappears between the headline, body, chart, conclusion and repost. Review the meaning that survives these transformations, rather than treating fluent or technical language as proof.

## One claim, five checkpoints

1. **Extract:** Write each substantive claim as a checkable statement. Record subject, comparator, population or dataset, metric, time, operating conditions and excluded cases.
2. **Trace:** Identify primary documentation, independent measurement, vendor assertion or commentary. Multiple articles quoting one original announcement are not independent replication.
3. **Disambiguate:** Separate nearby concepts when the difference changes the conclusion. Supply an evidence-backed discriminating criterion and a meaningful counterexample; do not manufacture ambiguity for simple questions.
4. **Preserve:** Compare title, evidence, chart axes, conclusion and later summary. Check whether conditional observations have been silently upgraded to universal guarantees.
5. **Review:** Present the smallest accurate summary that retains decision-relevant limitations, uncertainty and reversal conditions. An appropriate technical vocabulary may suit specialist audiences, but terminology is not a credential or proof.

### Example: format reliability is not answer accuracy

- Source-limited statement: 'The tool returns one of the predefined labels in the current schema.'
- Unsupported compression: 'The tool cannot make a mistake.'
- Faithful summary: 'Its output format is constrained; whether the chosen label is correct requires separate evaluation.'

Likewise, a specific test's latency improvement cannot be restated as universal performance across all applications without matched comparisons. A model demo is not a deployed and independently validated safety system.

## When analyzing a published model output

The output may have been shaped by task instructions, retrieval, platform format, presentation templates, adapter orchestration, human selection or editing. One polished post cannot establish the underlying model family, proprietary architecture, private prompts or internal reasoning sequence. Treat observed writing patterns, compatible generating mechanisms and verified implementation as separate levels. A multipage article and its own comments count as a linked source event, not several independent experiments.

For longitudinal observation, compare unrelated subjects and audiences, record original inputs where authorized, look for invariants under rewriting, and count disconfirming cases. Tests on your own model do not identify another author's hidden system. Respect privacy and access controls; do not reconstruct protected internal assets.
