# Safety & Misuse Boundaries — Model 1 v1.0

## Safety position

Model 1 v1.0 is a **first-person-only human-controlled judgment guardrail**. It helps a person separate evidence from uncertainty and detect whether their own next step requires slowing down, freezing, escalating, or exiting.

It is not designed to analyze or control other people.

## Mandatory boundaries

### 1. First-person only

Permitted use concerns the user's own:

- proposed next action;
- evidence and unknowns;
- boundary;
- cost exposure;
- reversibility;
- decision to proceed, slow, freeze, escalate, or exit.

Not permitted:

- diagnosing, labelling, or profiling another person;
- mapping another person's weaknesses or likely reactions;
- deciding what another person “really is”;
- producing tactics to gain compliance or emotional leverage;
- using `+ / 0 / -` or Green / Yellow / Red to rank people.

### 2. No weaponization

This repository must not be used for:

- coercion;
- manipulation;
- persuasion optimization;
- pressure sequencing;
- exploitation of vulnerability;
- harassment or retaliation;
- automated human-risk scoring.

A framework intended to detect whether **I should stop** must not be converted into a system for deciding **how to move, punish, exclude, or rank someone else**.

### 3. Evidence direction is not authority

The public evidence direction is:

- `+` — present evidence supports the proposed path;
- `0` — evidence is insufficient, mixed, or unresolved;
- `-` — present evidence opposes the proposed path.

These states do not authorize execution, approval, denial, punishment, or action against another person.

`0` must remain unresolved. It must not be silently converted into approval or rejection merely to complete a workflow.

### 4. Risk signals are not automated authority

A Green, Yellow, or Red signal does not authorize a real-world action.

The protocol must not be wired into a system that automatically:

- sends messages;
- changes access or permissions;
- spends money;
- commits code or publishes content;
- makes legal, medical, employment, financial, or safety decisions;
- triggers reports, blocks, punishments, or actions against another person.

### 5. Do not wash away hard boundaries

Gray analysis is appropriate for partial or mixed evidence. It is not permission to weaken:

- safety stops;
- legal or authorization requirements;
- privacy or consent limits;
- contract, payment, or specification facts;
- required validation steps;
- non-negotiable personal boundaries.

Complexity must not be used to erase responsibility.

### 6. Not a professional substitute

This repository is not therapy, counseling, medical advice, legal advice, emergency response, financial advice, regulatory approval, or professional certification.

Where professional or emergency systems are required, the protocol may at most help the user pause, preserve evidence, and organize questions. It does not replace qualified professionals or emergency services.

### 7. Privacy and public-data boundary

Do not put identifiable private medical, financial, employment, relationship, family, location, or safety information into public tools or public repository material.

Public examples must be de-identified, composite where appropriate, and reduced to reusable structure.

The ability to infer information does not create permission to disclose, track, or continue probing.

### 8. Public/private architecture boundary

Public v1.0 must not expose or reconstruct:

- private cases or identifiable formation history;
- private observer packs;
- thresholds or calibration chains;
- detailed market, interaction, allocation, or pacing models;
- anti-poisoning implementation internals;
- hidden runtime packs;
- autonomous execution or orchestration logic.

## Invalid outputs

Any output is invalid if it:

- makes claims about another person's diagnosis, motives, value, or ranking;
- tells the user how to control or pressure another person;
- treats evidence direction as approval or denial authority;
- treats a risk signal as permission to execute;
- fills important unknowns with guesses;
- hides a hard boundary behind vague nuance;
- encourages irreversible action without human review;
- expands the protocol into an all-purpose life system;
- exposes private narrative or private-engine material for illustration.

## Human Final Gate

Every consequential use requires a responsible human to review:

- the proposed action;
- verified facts;
- inferences and unknowns;
- evidence direction;
- boundary and consequence;
- reversibility and rollback;
- the Green / Yellow / Red signal;
- ownership of the final decision.

No Human Final Gate, no consequential execution.

## Hard rule

> Keep what helps a person identify whether they should stop.  
> Remove what helps a person influence, push, rank, punish, or control others.
